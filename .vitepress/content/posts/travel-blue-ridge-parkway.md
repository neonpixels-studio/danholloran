I grew up thinking the East Coast didn't have scenery in the same league as the West, which is the kind of thing you believe until you drive the Blue Ridge Parkway for the first time. The road winds along the spine of the Blue Ridge Mountains for 469 miles, connecting Shenandoah in Virginia to Great Smoky Mountains in North Carolina, and there are no stoplights, no commercial vehicles, and an overlook every mile and a half on average. It's a national parkway, which means the whole thing is managed for the experience of driving it. That makes a difference.

The parkway was built during the Great Depression as a public works project, taking more than 50 years to complete — the last section, the Linn Cove Viaduct on Grandfather Mountain in North Carolina, wasn't finished until 1987. That viaduct is one of those pieces of engineering that earns a stop on its own: a curved concrete bridge that wraps around the face of the mountain to avoid disturbing the ecosystem on the slope. From the overlook below, watching traffic move around the curve with the valley spreading out beyond it, it looks like something from a science fiction landscape.

The Appalachians are old mountains. The Blue Ridge peaks in this stretch are among the oldest exposed rock formations in North America, worn down from what were once Himalayan-scale heights over hundreds of millions of years. That age gives the landscape a rounded, layered quality very different from the Rockies — the ridges fold into each other, blue with haze in the distance, in a way that makes the name make immediate sense. Humpback Rocks and Mabry Mill are classic stops; the Craggy Gardens section near Asheville blooms with Catawba rhododendrons in June in a way that seems almost implausible.

Spring and fall are the obvious seasons — wildflowers in April and May, fall color in October — but the drive in any season has something. There's no fee, no reservation required, just a tank of gas and some time. The speed limit is 45 mph and the curves keep you honest. The point is not to get anywhere fast. The point is the road.
